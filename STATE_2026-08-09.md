## 1. The goals

**What it is:** a deep-time diorama. A 96×96 isometric world where
civilizations rise, spread, fight, cross oceans, and fall over geological time,
watched from a fixed vantage. Not a game — there is no win condition, no
player, no city-builder loop. Closer to a screensaver you glance at, or a
window. **The land is the protagonist; civilizations are weather.**

**Standing aesthetic direction** (from the run briefs, in your words):

- **Painterly, not photographic.** A watercolor-and-ink eye. Soft edges over
  hard edges, washes over gradients, atmosphere over precision. Tang dynasty
  landscape, Annie Dillard's deep-time prose, quiet-register Ghibli, *Future
  Boy Conan* skies. Avoid hyper-saturation, anime-bright, "epic" lighting,
  photorealism. **Quiet wins.**
- **Light as a medium.** The sky isn't a backdrop, it's a layer that touches
  everything. Noon and dusk should differ in *what color the shadows are*, not
  just in brightness.
- **Calm has texture of its own.** The world between events must be worth
  watching.
- **Time at multiple scales**, all present, none announced: day/night in
  minutes, seasons in tens of minutes, eras across a world's life.
- **The land remembers.** Scars persist, ruins fade slowly, silt stays, a
  plagued region stays pale.

**The two acceptance tests you set**, still the right ones:

1. *The calm test* — a new viewer watches 2 minutes with nothing happening. Are
   they still looking?
2. *The aftermath test* — after a catastrophe, can a viewer point at the screen
   and say "that's where it hit" 30+ seconds later?

**Working mode:** you're the taste/design lead — you watch the sim and make the
visual calls; the agent implements. You want small diffs, not whole files.
Feelings ("looks like noise", "too sudden") are design signals to diagnose, not
literal specs. Tuning constants is cheap and is the first move; architecture is
the last.

**Hard constraints:** no network calls in production (offline-capable at
runtime), no telemetry/ads/analytics, audio muted by default, and the sim must
hold 30 ticks/sec — a new effect that visibly costs frames gets simplified or
cut.

> **Overtaken 2026-08-19:** the "no telemetry/ads/analytics" clause no longer
> holds. Privacy-conscious analytics shipped in PR #3 — cookie-free, DNT/GPC
> respected, no seeds, URLs or world data sent. See `ANALYTICS.md` and
> `public/privacy/index.html`. The rest of the list stands.

## 2. How it works

Vanilla TypeScript + Vite + **Pixi.js v8**. Two runtime dependencies (`pixi.js`,
`simplex-noise`). No backend, no router, no framework. `index.html` is empty —
it loads `src/main.ts`, which builds everything including the HUD DOM.

### Modules

| File | Size | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `src/main.ts` | ~6.7k lines | Pixi bootstrap, the layer stack, the tick loop, ~40 story/render subsystems, HUD, narration log, civ bars, debug menu |
| `src/sim.ts` | ~2.2k | The simulation. Pure — **no Pixi imports, ever**. Tiles, civs, cities, eras, expeditions, catastrophes, terraforming |
| `src/atmosphere.ts` | ~1.6k | Sky, day/night, weather, seasons, scars, celestial events, planetary curvature |
| `src/biomes.ts` | ~170 | Seeded simplex terrain (3 noise channels + island falloff) and rivers |
| `src/naturalWonders.ts` | ~140 | Permanent, seed-placed land landmarks |
| `src/names.ts` | ~80 | Per-era syllable pools; names evolve across eras |
| `src/iso.ts` | ~150 | Iso projection, diamond drawing, color helpers |
| `src/audio.ts` | ~130 | Procedural Web Audio. No files, no libraries |

### The render pipeline (the fact that governs all new visual work)

The `world` container does **not** draw to the screen directly. Every frame it
renders into a **RenderTexture**, which is then drawn through a bent
**MeshPlane** whose vertices remap the world's upper silhouette onto a horizon
arc. That's why the diamond reads as a planet's limb rather than a game board.

Consequence, and it is the single most useful thing to know before proposing a
feature: **anything drawn in world space bends with the planet for free**
(scars, roads, armies, boats, labels, ground weather). **Anything drawn in
screen space does not** (sky, glaze, dread tint, vignette, flash, stars). New
features must pick a side deliberately.

Layer order, bottom to top: sky → star dome → biome layer (cached texture) →
water glitter → farmland → tile-tint overlays (cached) → roads/trails →
buildings → power/energy → conflict → war → wonders → skylines → boats/air →
city markers → labels → weather (cloud shadow, fog, storms) → curvature mesh →
fullscreen glaze/dread/vignette/flash.

### The tick loop

Fixed 30 ticks/sec on a `deltaMS` accumulator. Each tick `step()` returns
`{changes, events, biomeChanges}` and the renderer reacts:

- Changed tiles get their overlay, building sprite, and farmland refreshed.
- Biome changes crossfade over the cached base, then commit to the cache.
- Events fan out into narration, audio, atmosphere (scars, impact, epicenter).
- Every `SIM.worldCycleTicks` the world is unmade: blackout beat, reroll,
  cataclysm line, fade up on a new land.

### The rhythms — deliberately layered

| Register | Period | Knob |
|---|---|---|
| Sim tick | 1/30 s | `ticksPerSecond` |
| Day/night | 6 min | `ATMOS.day.cycleSeconds` |
| Seasonal year | 20 min | `ATMOS.season` |
| Era | ~2.5–3 min each | derived from the world cycle |
| World life → cataclysm → reroll | ~17 min | **`SIM.worldCycleTicks = 30000`** |

`worldCycleTicks` is the single deep-time knob. Era progress is rescaled
against `eraReferenceCycle` so the full neolithic→post climb always lands at
~74% of a world's life whatever length you pick. 100k ≈ the original 55-minute
deep time; below ~20k the eras blur.

### The sim in one paragraph

Tiles are `wild | cleared | built | ruin`. Civs are agents with `constitution`,
`fortune`, `vitality`, an ambition cap, a phase (`rising → stable → declining →
dead`), an era fixed at birth, and a list of cities (capital first). Effective
strength drives spread and conquest; its inverse drives decay, amplified at the
periphery and for isolated tiles, damped near the capital, and capped at one
loss per civ per tick so nothing collapses in a blink. Catastrophe pressure
accumulates with a noisy multiplier, brews a type and severity, fires three
escalating omens, then lands — leaving scars, ruins that remember their era, and
sometimes new terrain. New civs inherit era from nearby ruins or from the world
era floor, so history ratchets forward.

### Where the knobs live

Everything tuneable is grouped and commented: `SIM`, `CATASTROPHE`, `CITY` in
`sim.ts`; `ATMOS` in `atmosphere.ts` with sub-blocks (`composition`, `day`,
`curve`, `weather`, `season`, `celestial`, `glitter`, `stars`, `landLight`,
`shimmer`, `camera`, `storm`, `events`, `era`, `scar`); `DENSITY`, `QUALITY`,
`ERA_HEIGHT_RANGE`, `ERA_SAT_MULT` and per-subsystem blocks in `main.ts`.

### Watching and debugging

- `npm run dev`. `npm run build` typechecks with `noUnusedLocals` — dead
  bindings break the build. No test runner, no linter.
- HUD: seed, tick, FPS (color-coded), reroll, reset sim, sound, gfx quality,
  skip (5000 ticks), a debug menu that force-fires any event/structure/wonder,
  civ bars, narration log, a real-world clock plus the deep-time year.
- URL/localStorage: `?seed=`, `?mres=`, `?rt=`; quality persists.
- In-page handles: `__sim`, `__atmos`, `__atmosphere` (time/curvature
  scrubbers), `__energy()`, `__wonders`, `__perf`.
- `scripts/` holds ~45 Playwright harnesses. Headless works for large features
  and "does the code path run" checks. **It cannot judge city- or war-scale
  visuals** — there's no camera zoom, the whole globe is always in frame, and
  units are a few pixels. That tuning has to happen on your screen.

## 3. What's actually in the world today

Grouped, so gaps are visible.

**Land & time** — 3-channel terrain with island falloff; rivers that flow
strictly downhill; living biomes that breathe (forests creep and retreat, sea
level oscillates) on unclaimed tiles only; seed-placed natural wonders
(volcano, crater lake, monolith, rainbow hills, karst spires, salt flat) that
attract or repel settlement.

**Sky & light** — 9-keyframe day/night with a night legibility cap; sun and
moon arcs with color temperature; water glitter along the light path; star dome
that rotates and is occluded by the planet; constellations named by the first
civ of each era; comets, eclipses, auroras, meteor showers; cloud shadows and
mist on a shared wandering wind; a 20-minute seasonal palette; per-era air
(neolithic clarity → industrial soot → modern wash → post violet); traveling
storms with rain and lightning; planetary curvature and a limb haze.

**Civilization** — territory, cities with prominence and names, capital
succession, breakaway colonies, ocean expeditions and colonization, wonders that
outlive their builders, roads that evolve trail → rail → glow-thread, era
settlement tells (walls, smokestacks, broadcast masts), night city lights, era
smoke, farmland, energy farms, power lines, megastructures, causeways,
lighthouses, boats, river barges, caravans, planes, rockets, satellites, an
orbital ring, nomad bands that wander before settling, herds, bird flocks,
festivals, ghost echoes of dead names.

**Catastrophe & consequence** — five types (plague, asteroid, flood,
earthquake, volcano) with brewing, three omen stages in era-flavored voices,
ambient dread that recolors sky and wind, impact punctuation with shake and
flash, and persistent painterly scars per type. Geology that permanently
changes the map: rifting, island birth, land bridges, crater lakes, fertile
volcanic ash. Wars with visible armies, banners and sieges that re-arm by era.
Faiths that spread city to city as a golden tide. Droughts, floods, deltas,
wildfires.

**Voice** — every line routes through one priority queue (high: disasters,
deaths, wonders, world-shaping geology; normal: births, breakaways, festivals;
low: war churn, ambient whispers, chronicle), with dedup and a global floor
between war lines. Measured ~3.2 lines/min. Procedural audio, muted by default.

## 4. Current state

- **Branch/deploy:** `main` is now the real tip (166 commits, fast-forwarded and
  pushed 2026-08-09). Both run branches are backed up on the remote. The Vercel
  project is GitHub-connected: pushing `main` auto-deploys, and
  `the-land-chi.vercel.app` is verified byte-identical to the current tip.
- **Uncommitted:** one change in `main.ts` — war rendering pooled into a
  `Graphics` per battle, pivoted at its own front centre, scaled by
  `WAR_SCALE = 1.7` so a clash grows in place from one knob. Compiles, unshipped,
  waiting on your eye at real resolution.
- **Performance is the live constraint.** The frame is **fill-bound, not
  object-bound** — hiding all ~11k building sprites changes nothing measurable.
  The RT pass plus the curvature mesh is roughly half the frame. Caching the
  tile-tint layer bought ~20 → ~30 fps at a developed era; buildings can't be
  cached (they animate every frame). The remaining levers are canvas resolution
  (the `gfx:` button) and turning curvature off, which costs the whole globe
  look. **Do not re-propose sprite batching** — it's been measured twice.
- **Known soft spots:** late-era worlds are the expensive ones; war-scale
  visuals are unverified; frame-rate-coupled easing means slow machines settle
  slower.

## 5. Invariants a proposal must not break

- No Pixi in `sim.ts`; the renderer stays decoupled.
- `step()` reads an immutable snapshot, writes the live tile.
- The `fadedDeadCivs` repaint pass — the per-tile change list misses dead-civ
  tiles, so their fade is driven separately.
- `maxDecaysPerCivPerTick`, capital protection, breakaway throttle, era
  inheritance at birth, name-memory radius, seed persistence.
- Biome and tile-tint layers are cached textures; anything that rewrites tiles
  continuously fights those caches (this is why sea-level epochs were parked).
- No network at runtime.

## 6. Candidate next features

Ranked by my read of payoff against the calm test and cost against the frame
budget. This is the menu to sharpen, not a plan.

**A. Ice ages** — SHIPPED 2026-08-09 (`6ec7e31`). Original note kept for the
reasoning behind it:
Ice creeps from both poles over ~15–20 min; the habitable band narrows toward
the equator; civs retreat and decay in the cold; then it recedes. Cheap as a
creeping overlay from the screen edges — no per-tile biome rewrite, so it
doesn't fight the caches — with the sim reading a single "ice line" so civs
actually respond. *Why it's strong:* it's a planetary rhythm on top of the era
arc, it's slow (pure calm-test material), and it makes the map's geography
matter. *Risk:* the visual must stay a wash, not a white slab.

**B. The dying world before the cataclysm** — note: a blight system already
exists (`BLIGHT`, drains the land toward grey as the cataclysm nears); this
would be extending it, not building it.
Extend pollution into a slow global color-drain spreading outward from
developed regions in a world's final minutes, so the planet visibly exhausts
before it ends. *Why:* the cataclysm currently arrives as an event rather than
an ending; this gives the world's death a third act. *Cost:* low — it's a tint
front over existing pollution machinery. *Risk:* grey is the easiest way to
make a beautiful thing ugly.

**C. Sea-level epochs**
Slow global rise and fall; coastlines breathe, isthmuses drown and return.
*Parked twice* because continuous reclassification fights the biome texture
cache. Worth revisiting only with a cheaper approach — e.g. a shoreline wash
layer that moves without touching the cached tiles, accepting that the
underlying biomes stay put.

**D. Real-sky sync** *(no network required)*
Phase-lock the land's seasonal year to the real calendar, and drive moon
brightness and night darkness from the true moon phase — both are pure math
from the system clock. *Why:* the screensaver quietly shares your sky.
*Cost:* very low. *Risk:* essentially none; it's the cheapest charm in the list.

**E. Tectonic uplift**
Mountain ranges rise slowly along fault lines across a world's life; terrain
grows rugged with age. Uses the existing terraform queue. *Why:* the land
itself has an arc, which is the project's whole thesis. *Cost:* medium — it
does rewrite terrain, so it costs cache invalidation.

**F. Small, cheap, unshipped**
Ocean tide breathing at coastlines; star reflections on water at deep night;
expedition wakes; per-biome seasonal tint (forests amber harder than grass);
cloud *bodies* over open water only.

**G. Housekeeping worth doing at some point**
Convert frame-coupled easing to time-based; rewrite `CLAUDE.md`, which now
describes a project three times smaller than the real one; decide whether
`main.ts` at 6.7k lines should be split by subsystem (it's navigable, but it's
the only file that ever gets edited).

**Not recommended:** live weather or any other network-fed data — it breaks the
offline constraint and pulls modernity into a deep-time texture. Also, sprite
batching for performance: measured, twice, as not the bottleneck.

## 7. If you're briefing a collaborator on this

The proposals that fit this project answer four questions:

1. **Which register does it live in?** Frame-scale motion, day, season, era, or
   the world's whole life. The project is strongest when a new thing occupies a
   register that isn't crowded — slow ones are underused.
2. **World space or screen space?** World space bends with the planet and is
   usually right for anything touching the ground; screen space is for light and
   air.
3. **What does it cost per frame?** The budget is fill, not objects. A
   fullscreen blended quad is expensive; ten thousand small sprites are not.
   Effects should gate themselves to `visible = false` when negligible.
4. **What are its knobs?** Ten well-named, well-grouped constants that respond
   beautifully beat a system with good defaults and nothing to turn. That's the
   collaboration surface — the agent builds the system, you tune the values.

A good proposal here is one paragraph of *what the viewer sees*, then the
knobs, then the cheapest implementation that gets there. Not a feature list.
